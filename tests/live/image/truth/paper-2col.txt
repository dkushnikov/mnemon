Bounded Recall: Retrieval Budgets for Long-Context Agents
Alex Placeholder, Blair Example
Example Institute of Technology
Abstract
Long-context language models invite a simple strategy: retrieve more and let the model sort it out. We show that this strategy has a cost that grows faster than its benefit. On three question answering benchmarks, raising the number of retrieved passages from 8 to 32 improves recall by 3.8 points but raises the rate of misattributed quotations from 4.1% to 11.7%. We propose a retrieval budget, a fixed cap on passages enforced after ranking, and report the dropped passages as a diagnostic. The budget recovers most of the accuracy at a quarter of the token cost.
1 Introduction
Agents that answer questions from a private library face a trade-off between coverage and focus. A wider retrieval step finds more of the relevant evidence, but every passage it returns competes for the attention of the model that writes the answer.
Prior work treats the number of passages as a tuning constant. We argue that it is a budget, and that a budget should be stated, enforced and audited like any other resource of the system.
Our contributions are threefold. First, we measure the effect of the passage count on recall, latency and quotation accuracy across three benchmarks. Second, we define a retrieval budget and a simple enforcement rule. Third, we show that recording the dropped passages makes failures easy to diagnose.
2 Related Work
Retrieval augmented generation has been studied extensively. Most systems fix the passage count once and never revisit it.
